# Discovery Call Script

**Format:** Free 30-minute video call  
**Goal:** Understand the prospect's business and pain, assess fit, and close toward a Tier 1 Assessment (or identify a mismatch early)  
**Tone:** Conversational and curious, not scripted or salesy — you're a consultant on an intake call, not a rep running a pitch

---

## Before the Call

**Review the Intake Form** — Note the 1–2 pain points they mentioned and any
prior AI experience. Have a specific follow-up question ready for each.

**Have open:** A blank doc or notepad to capture key phrases verbatim. Direct quotes
from prospects often become the exact language in the AI Agent Opportunity Report.

**Set your context:** You're not trying to close a deal on this call. You're trying
to understand whether working together is the right move — and to help the prospect
feel that distinction.

---

## Call Structure (30 Minutes)

| Phase | Time | Purpose |
|-------|------|---------|
| Open + rapport | 3–4 min | Set tone, lower defenses |
| Business context | 5–7 min | Understand what they do and how |
| Pain excavation | 8–10 min | Find the real friction |
| AI readiness check | 4–5 min | Assess data, tools, openness |
| Budget + decision | 2–3 min | Qualify without pressure |
| Close | 3–4 min | Recommend a next step |

---

## Phase 1 — Open + Rapport (3–4 min)

**Opener:**

> "Thanks for making time — I'm looking forward to this.
> Before we jump in, did you get a chance to look at the intake form I sent?
> [Yes → great / No → no problem, we'll cover the same ground together]
>
> Let me just say upfront: the goal of this call isn't to pitch you on anything.
> It's to understand your business well enough to figure out whether there's
> a real opportunity here — for both of us. If there's not a fit, I'll tell
> you that. Does that work?"

*[Pause — let them respond. Most people visibly relax here.]*

**Quick rapport questions (pick one, don't over-invest):**

- "How did you hear about us?" *(Useful for sourcing data and relationship warmth)*
- "How long have you been running [Company]?"
- "What's keeping you busiest right now?"

---

## Phase 2 — Business Context (5–7 min)

**Goal:** Understand what the business actually does at an operational level —
not the marketing version, but the workflow version.

**Questions (use 2–3, don't read them all):**

> "Walk me through what a typical week looks like for your team.
> What are the things that happen over and over?"

> "How does work come in — through a form, email, phone call, referral?
> And then what happens after that?"

> "When you think about the parts of the business that are running well —
> what's working? I want to understand the baseline."

**Listen for:**
- Volume: "we get 50 of those a week" → high-value automation candidate
- Manual handoffs: "someone emails it to [person] who puts it in [system]"
- Data touchpoints: what systems they mentioned, which ones don't talk to each other
- Decision points: where a human has to approve or judge something

**Take note of exact phrases.** "We're the ones holding everything together" is
a better report headline than "there are integration gaps."

---

## Phase 3 — Pain Excavation (8–10 min)

**Goal:** Get past the surface frustration to the specific, costly, recurring pain.
This is the most important part of the call.

**Opener:**

> "So — in the intake form you mentioned [specific thing they wrote].
> Tell me more about that. How often does that happen, and what does it actually
> cost you when it does?"

**Excavation questions:**

> "If I gave you back 5 hours a week for your team,
> where would you most want those hours to go?"

> "Is there a task that you keep meaning to fix — the kind of thing you put up
> with because there's always something more urgent — but it's slowly driving
> people crazy?"

> "Where do mistakes happen? What does the rework look like when something
> goes wrong in that process?"

> "You mentioned [Tool A] and [Tool B]. Is there a point where information
> has to move between those manually? What does that look like?"

> "What would have to be true for you to say 'this year we solved that problem'?"
> *(This surfaces the success vision — critical for scoping.)*

**Signs you've found a real opportunity:**
- They get more animated or specific when describing it
- They can tell you frequency, volume, or time cost without thinking
- They've tried to fix it before and couldn't / didn't have time
- Multiple people are involved in the pain (cross-functional = higher value)

**If they're vague:**

> "Can you walk me through the last time that happened — step by step?
> What did [person] do first?"

---

## Phase 4 — AI Readiness Check (4–5 min)

**Goal:** Assess whether the business is realistically positioned for an agent
implementation — without making them feel tested.

**Data and tools:**

> "The tools you mentioned — do they connect to each other, or does your team
> end up being the bridge between them?"

> "Is there data sitting somewhere that nobody's really using — a spreadsheet,
> a folder full of reports — that might actually be useful if someone organized it?"

> "If I wanted to connect to your [CRM / email / project management tool] via
> an API, do you know if that's something your account supports?"
> *(Don't expect them to know — the point is their reaction: openness vs. anxiety)*

**Team openness:**

> "If we built something that took a task off your team's plate,
> what's your read on how they'd react? Are they excited about that
> kind of thing, or more cautious?"

> "Have you had any bad experiences with software implementations before —
> where something got rolled out and nobody ended up using it?"
> *(This surfaces change management risk early.)*

**Prior AI experience:**

> "You mentioned [whatever they said in the form]. What's your honest take
> on that experience? Did it change how you think about this stuff?"

---

## Phase 5 — Budget and Decision (2–3 min)

**Goal:** Qualify budget and decision-making without making it feel like a qualifying
conversation. Frame around helping them plan, not gatekeeping.

**Budget:**

> "I want to give you a realistic picture of what engagement with us looks like cost-wise,
> so you're not surprised. Our starting point — a structured AI Readiness Assessment —
> runs $2,500 to $5,000 depending on scope. Is that in the neighborhood of what you
> were thinking, or would that be a stretch?"

*[Let them respond — don't fill the silence. Their first answer tells you a lot.]*

If they hesitate:

> "That's fair to pause on. It's worth knowing that we credit the Assessment fee toward
> any implementation work we do together. So it's not just a consulting fee —
> it's a down payment on the map."

**Decision-making:**

> "If we talked after this call and I said 'here's what I'd recommend and why' —
> who would need to be part of that conversation before a decision could move forward?"

*[Note: if there's a partner, board member, or investor not on the call, they need to be
loop in. Ask whether a second call with that person would be appropriate.]*

---

## Phase 6 — Close (3–4 min)

**Goal:** Give them a clear, honest recommendation and a specific next step.
Don't equivocate. They came to you for guidance.

**If it's a clear fit:**

> "Here's what I'd recommend. Based on what you've told me — especially around [specific pain]
> — there are 2–3 workflows that I'd want to look at closely in a structured assessment.
> That's where we'd start.
>
> The Assessment takes 1–2 weeks, runs $[X] for your scope, and at the end you get a
> prioritized report — what to automate, in what order, and what it would cost to build.
> Even if we don't work together beyond that, you'll have a clear picture.
>
> Does that feel like the right next step?"

**If it's a partial fit / needs more thought:**

> "I think there's something real here, but I want to be straight with you:
> [specific thing] needs to be figured out before it would make sense to invest in an
> Assessment. Here's what I'd suggest: [action — e.g., talk to your IT vendor about API
> access / run a data cleanup first / revisit in Q3 after your busy season].
> Can I follow up with you in [timeframe]?"

**If it's not a fit:**

> "I want to be honest with you — based on what you've described, I don't think
> this is the right moment for an AI agent engagement. The reason is [specific reason —
> e.g., your team is at capacity for a change initiative right now / the workflows
> you've described are too dependent on unstructured data that would need cleanup first].
>
> What I'd suggest instead is [referral / alternative approach]. I'm happy to make an
> introduction if that's useful."

## After the Call

**Within 24 hours:**

Send a brief follow-up email. Template:

> Subject: Notes from our call — next step

> [Name],
>
> Thanks for the time today. Here are the key things I took away from our conversation:
>
> - [Specific pain point 1, in their words]
> - [Specific pain point 2]
> - [Workflow or system they mentioned that stood out]
>
> Based on that, my recommendation is [specific next step — Assessment scope + price,
> follow-up timeline, referral, etc.].
>
> [If recommending Assessment:] I'll send over a proposal this week. If you want to
> move forward, we can kick off as soon as [earliest start date].
>
> [If not ready yet:] I'll follow up on [specific date] as we discussed.
>
> Feel free to reach out with questions in the meantime.
>
> — [Name]

**Log in CRM:** Add contact, company, pain points, next step, and follow-up date.

## Handling Common Objections

**"I need to think about it."**
> "Of course. What would be most helpful to think through? Is it the scope,
> the timing, or something else? I want to make sure you have what you need
> to make a good call."

**"The budget is too high."**
> "That's fair. Can I ask — is it that the dollar amount is a stretch right now,
> or that you're not sure yet whether the return would be worth it? Those are
> different problems and I'd address them differently."

**"We already tried [tool] and it didn't work."**
> "What happened? I want to understand — was it the tool, the implementation,
> or the workflow that was the issue? That context actually helps me a lot."

**"We don't have the technical capability internally."**
> "That's exactly why we handle the full build and hand you a working system
> with documentation your team can manage. You don't need an internal developer
> for this. What I do need is someone on your team who can test it and give
> feedback during the build — usually an hour or two a week."

**"We're too busy right now."**
> "I hear that. When would be the right time to revisit? And in the meantime —
> is the busy season making the problem you described better or worse? Sometimes
> the best time to fix a bottleneck is right after the crunch."

---

## Notes on Tone

- You're a trusted advisor on an intake call, not a salesperson running a script.
- Silence is your friend. Ask a question and wait.
- Use their exact words when summarizing back to them. It signals you actually listened.
- It's fine to say "I don't know" — and then say "I'll find out before the proposal."
- Honest disqualification ("this isn't the right fit yet") builds more long-term
  business than pushing an engagement that will disappoint.
- The goal isn't to get a yes on this call. It's to earn the right to a proposal.

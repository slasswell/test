## 1. Executive Summary

### What This Business Does and For Whom

This is a boutique AI agent consulting and implementation firm serving small and medium-sized businesses (SMBs) — companies with 5 to 200 employees in industries like professional services, healthcare administration, construction and trades, retail, and real estate. We help these businesses move from AI curiosity to measurable operational improvement: first by identifying where AI agents will create real, durable value in their specific workflows, and then by building, deploying, and supporting those agents.

The engagement model has two intertwined functions. **Discovery Consulting** produces a structured, prioritized roadmap — the AI Agent Opportunity Report — telling a client exactly what to automate, in what order, and why. **Delivery and Implementation** executes the roadmap: designing, building, testing, and deploying working AI agents integrated into the client's environment.

This is not a software product. It is a professional services business grounded in process rigor, honest communication, and practical results.

### The Founder's Relevant Background

The founder brings a background in enterprise program management, process design, and client-facing delivery across complex technology environments. Core competencies directly applicable to this model include: structured workflow analysis, stakeholder communication, cross-functional project governance, and the ability to translate technical concepts into plain operational language. This background allows the business to deliver enterprise-quality methodology at a scale and price point accessible to SMBs — the businesses that have historically been underserved by both large consulting firms and by AI software vendors.

### Revenue Model Overview

Revenue flows from four service tiers:
- **Tier 1 — AI Readiness Assessment:** Fixed-fee discovery engagement ($2,500–$5,000)
- **Tier 2 — Agent Blueprint:** Detailed implementation planning ($5,000–$12,000)
- **Tier 3 — Agent Build & Deploy:** Full agent construction and launch ($10,000–$40,000+)
- **Tier 4 — Retainer / Agent Maintenance:** Ongoing support and iteration ($1,500–$4,000/month)

Year 1 revenue target: **$150,000–$250,000**, with retainer revenue growing as a share of total by Q3.

### 12-Month Vision

By the end of the first year: the business has completed 8–12 discovery engagements, built and deployed AI agents for 4–6 clients, holds 4–6 active monthly retainers, and has established a replicable delivery model with documented templates and at least one trusted subcontractor relationship. The founder has become a recognizable voice in SMB AI adoption through LinkedIn content and at least one local speaking engagement.

## 2. Problem & Market Opportunity

### The SMB AI Adoption Gap

AI is not a secret. Small business owners have heard about ChatGPT. They've read the articles. Many have even tried something — a Zapier automation, a chatbot, an AI writing tool. But there is a profound gap between awareness and adoption, and it is widest for businesses without an internal technology team.

The typical SMB operator faces these overlapping obstacles:

- **No internal AI expertise.** They don't have a CTO or an AI engineer. They have a bookkeeper, a project coordinator, and an operations manager who all wear three hats already.
- **Too many tool choices, too little guidance.** The automation and AI landscape is fragmented and loud. Every platform claims to be the right one. There is no trusted guide.
- **Unclear ROI.** SMB owners operate on tight margins. They will not invest in something they can't quantify. Without someone to build the ROI case, they stay on the sideline.
- **Previous tool disappointments.** Many have tried software tools that were oversold, underimplemented, or abandoned after onboarding. Their default is skepticism.
- **Fear of disrupting what works.** Many SMBs run lean, well-worn processes. The risk of breaking something that already functions — even imperfectly — outweighs the theoretical upside of automation.

The result: most SMBs are leaving significant efficiency, capacity, and margin on the table — not because they can't afford to act, but because no one has helped them see clearly where to act and how.

### Why Consultants Matter More Than Software Vendors Here

Software vendors solve for adoption at scale. They build onboarding flows, help centers, and trial periods designed for a generic user. They cannot sit with a 30-person freight brokerage and understand how quotes are assembled, where the handoff to carriers breaks down, and what a working fix actually looks like in that specific environment.

A trusted consultant can. And in AI agent adoption, this distinction is critical: the agent itself is the easy part. The hard parts are identifying the right workflow to target, defining the boundary of what the agent should and should not do, integrating it with systems already in use, and getting the team to trust and use it consistently. None of those problems are solved by a software subscription.

This business competes not with software platforms, but with inertia and confusion. Our value is clarity and execution.

### Market Sizing

**Total Addressable Market (TAM)**  
U.S. AI consulting and implementation services across all business sizes: approximately $15–20 billion annually and growing 30%+ year over year as agentic AI capabilities mature.

**Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM)**  
SMBs (5–200 employees) actively seeking external help with AI adoption or automation. Estimated 800,000–1,200,000 businesses in the U.S. meet this profile, with median annual technology consulting spend of $5,000–$30,000. SAM: approximately $4–8 billion.

**Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM) — Year 1**  
One founder-led boutique firm operating in local and regional networks, with a digital presence. Realistically 8–15 clients in the first year, generating $150K–$250K gross revenue. This represents a fraction of 1% of the SAM — the growth constraint is founder capacity, not market size.

### Trends Supporting the Market

- **Agentic AI has crossed the threshold.** Models like Claude, GPT-4o, and Gemini can now reliably complete multi-step tasks with tool use — meaning AI agents are no longer experimental for well-defined workflows. The capability gap that kept SMBs waiting has closed.
- **Automation tool proliferation has created integration complexity.** Most SMBs now run 5–15 SaaS tools. Each one is a potential integration point for an agent, and also a potential point of confusion. The need for a guide who understands both the workflow and the tooling has never been higher.
- **SMB owners are under more cost and capacity pressure.** Labor costs are up. Hiring is harder. The ROI case for automation that frees 5–10 hours of staff time per week is increasingly obvious.
- **The enterprise-to-SMB technology diffusion cycle is accelerating.** Capabilities that took enterprise AI teams years to build are now accessible to anyone with an API key and implementation expertise. The timing window for a boutique SMB-focused AI firm is now.

## 4. Delivery Model

### Engagement Flow

Every client engagement follows the same logical arc, regardless of which tier they enter at:

```
Intake → Assessment (Tier 1) → Blueprint (Tier 2) → Build & Deploy (Tier 3) → Retainer (Tier 4)
         ↑                      ↑                     ↑
         Entry point for        Entry point for        Entry point for
         most clients           clients with a         clients with a
                                clear use case         clear build spec
```

**Intake (Pre-Engagement)**  
Every engagement begins with a 30-minute discovery call (free) and a written questionnaire sent to the client before the first paid session. The questionnaire covers: business overview, key workflows, tool stack, team structure, prior AI experience, budget range, and success criteria. This ensures the first paid session is productive.

**Assessment Phase**  
Sessions are semi-structured conversations with the people who actually do the work — not just the executives who commissioned the engagement. Workflow maps are built collaboratively in sessions, not inferred afterward. Clients review and correct maps before the report is written.

**Blueprint Phase**  
Requirements are validated in workshops, not assumed from the Assessment. The Blueprint is a living document during the engagement; the client sees drafts and shapes the output. The ROI model is built with the client's own numbers, not industry benchmarks.

**Build & Deploy Phase**  
Builds are iterative. The client sees a working draft of the agent (even if incomplete) by the end of week 2 of any build engagement — no big-reveal deliveries. Review rounds are structured: the client tests against a defined scenario list, not open-ended exploration.

**Retainer Phase**  
Monthly check-ins follow a consistent agenda: agent performance review, open issues log, and one "discovery" question to probe for new opportunities. This keeps the retainer proactive, not reactive.

### Tools and Platforms

Tool recommendations are made per use case, not per preference. The standard toolkit:

| Layer | Tools |
|-------|-------|
| AI / Reasoning | Claude API (Anthropic), OpenAI API, Gemini API |
| Workflow Automation | n8n (self-hosted or cloud), Make (Integromat), Zapier |
| Enterprise Automation | Tines, Microsoft Copilot Studio, Power Automate |
| Custom Build | Python (FastAPI, LangChain, LlamaIndex), Node.js |
| Databases | Airtable, Notion, PostgreSQL, Supabase |
| Integration / API | REST APIs, webhooks, OAuth 2.0 |
| Document Delivery | Notion, PDF, DOCX (client preference) |
| Project Management | Linear or Notion (shared with client) |

**Vendor-Agnostic Principle:** The firm does not accept referral fees or reseller commissions from any tool vendor. Recommendations are based solely on fit.

### Subcontractor Strategy

The founder remains the primary relationship and strategy owner on all engagements. Technical build work for complex Tier 3 projects may be subcontracted to trusted specialists (Python developers, integration engineers, prompt engineers) when scope exceeds founder capacity.

Subcontractor relationships are established before they are needed — not recruited mid-project. Each subcontractor is vetted for: technical competence, communication reliability, and comfort with direct client interaction (when appropriate). Subcontractors sign an NDA and work-for-hire agreement on first engagement.

Subcontractor costs are factored into project pricing at 20–30% of build revenue, depending on scope. The client is not billed separately for subcontractors — their cost is embedded in the fixed fee.

### Staying the Relationship Owner

Regardless of who is doing the technical build work, the founder:
- Runs all client-facing calls and workshops
- Reviews and approves all deliverables before client delivery
- Owns the SOW, the timeline, and the change order process
- Is the single point of contact for client questions and escalations

This is not a staff augmentation model. The founder's judgment, communication, and accountability are the product.

## 5. Go-to-Market Strategy

### Initial Channels

**1. Warm Referrals (Primary — Months 1–6)**  
The most reliable source of first clients is the founder's existing network. Target: former colleagues, past clients from prior consulting roles, business owners in professional networks, and peers who know the founder's caliber of work. Goal: 2 pilot clients from warm referrals in the first 90 days.

**2. LinkedIn (Ongoing)**  
LinkedIn is the single highest-leverage content channel for a B2B services business targeting SMB operators and the advisors who serve them (accountants, MSPs, business coaches). Posting cadence: 3–4 times per week. Content mix:
- "AI agent for [industry]" explainer posts (builds organic reach)
- Short takes on common automation mistakes SMBs make
- Behind-the-scenes process posts ("here's how we structure an assessment")
- Client result spotlights (anonymized until written permission obtained)

**3. Local Business Networks and Chamber of Commerce**  
Local credibility is an underrated acquisition channel for SMB consulting. Target: 1–2 local chamber of commerce memberships, attend 1 networking event per month, speak at 1–2 events in Year 1. Many SMBs prefer working with someone local or regionally familiar.

**4. Faith-Based Business Networks**  
This is a high-trust, referral-rich community the founder may be embedded in. Business owners in these networks tend to refer within the community heavily when trust is established. Authenticity is essential — this is not a sales channel to broadcast to, but a community to serve and be known within.

**5. Content Marketing — "AI Agent for [Industry]" Posts**  
A series of SEO-targeted blog posts and LinkedIn articles answering the specific questions SMB operators in the target verticals are searching for:
- "How can a construction company use AI agents?"
- "AI automation for healthcare admin staff"
- "What can AI do for a small real estate brokerage?"

These posts generate inbound interest from the exact audience the business serves and position the founder as a domain-literate advisor, not a generic AI vendor.

### First 3 Clients: How to Get Them

**Outreach Strategy**  
Identify 20–30 people in the founder's network who own or lead SMBs in the target verticals. Send a personal, specific message (not a mass email) that:
- Acknowledges the relationship
- Describes the business in one sentence
- Offers something of value first: a free AI Readiness Checklist, a 20-minute "is AI right for your business?" call, or a complimentary look at one of their workflows
- Does not pitch before it listens

**Discovery Call Script (Outline)**  
1. "Tell me what's happening in the business right now — what's taking more time or effort than it should?"  
2. "Have you tried any AI or automation tools? What happened?"  
3. "If I could hand you back 5 hours a week for your team, where would you most want them spent?"  
4. "Would it be useful to do a quick structured look at your workflows together? Here's what that would look like and what it costs."

**Pilot Concept**  
For the first 2 clients, offer the Tier 1 Assessment at a 40–50% discount ($1,500–$2,000) in exchange for:
- Permission to use the engagement as a case study (anonymized unless they agree to attribution)
- A testimonial if satisfied
- A referral conversation after delivery

This is not discounting forever — it is buying case studies and social proof, which are worth more than full-fee revenue in the first 90 days.

### Partnership Opportunities

**IT MSPs (Managed Service Providers)**  
MSPs manage the technology infrastructure of many SMBs. They are not building AI agents; they are often asked about it by clients. A referral relationship with 2–3 regional MSPs — "we send you overflow IT questions, you refer clients curious about AI" — can generate consistent warm leads. MSPs who refer clients that convert to a paid engagement receive a referral fee (suggested: 10% of Tier 1 engagement fee).

**Accountants and Bookkeepers**  
Accountants see the operational pain of their SMB clients up close — manual data entry, reconciliation errors, report-building inefficiency. Many of these are automatable. An accountant who understands what we do and trusts our approach is an extremely high-quality referral source.

**Business Coaches and Fractional Executives**  
Fractional COOs, CMOs, and business coaches often work with SMBs on operational improvement. AI agent automation fits directly into that work. A partnership where they handle strategy and we handle AI implementation creates a complementary offering.

**Business Brokers**  
Businesses preparing for acquisition are increasingly valued on operational efficiency and technology adoption. A broker who can say "these processes are partially automated and documented" is delivering more value to a seller. A referral relationship with 1–2 business brokers can surface motivated, operationally-focused clients.

## 7. Competitive Positioning

### Who Else Is Doing This

| Competitor Type | Examples | SMB Fit | Weakness |
|----------------|---------|---------|---------|
| Large consulting firms | Deloitte, Accenture, McKinsey | Very poor | Minimum engagements start at $150K+; culturally misaligned with SMBs; built for enterprise procurement |

| Freelancers on gig platforms | Upwork, Fiverr AI agents | Partial | No structured methodology; inconsistent quality; no ongoing accountability |
| Boutique AI agencies | Emerging category | Varies | Often enterprise-focused or product-specific; few with SMB fluency and PM rigor |

### Differentiators

**1. Methodology before tools.**  
Most competitors start with a tool and work backward. We start with the workflow, produce a defensible roadmap, and select tools that fit — not tools we sell.

**2. SMB fluency.**  
Our pricing, communication, and delivery cadence are built around how SMBs actually work: lean teams, limited IT support, decision-makers who are also operators. We don't require a project coordinator on the client side to work with us.

**3. Founder-led accountability.**  
The person the client meets in the sales call is the person who does the work and delivers the results. No bait-and-switch hand-offs to junior staff.

**4. Enterprise-quality project management.**  
Clear SOWs, milestone billing, shared project visibility, documented decisions, change order discipline. This is unusual at the boutique level and consistently mentioned by clients as a differentiator.

**5. You own everything.**  
Documentation, agent logic, integration diagrams — fully handed over at engagement close. Clients are never dependent on us to keep their systems running.

**6. Honest scope.**

## 8. Risk & Mitigation

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|--------|-----------|
| **Market education curve** — SMB owners don't yet understand what an AI agent is or why it's different from "getting ChatGPT" | High | Medium | Lead with outcomes, not technology. Discovery call script avoids AI jargon; Tier 1 Assessment is positioned as "finding where to save time," not "AI consulting" |
| **Scope creep on Tier 3 builds** | Medium | High | Strict SOW with defined deliverables; milestone billing with built-in review gates; change order process enforced from day one |
| **Client technical readiness gaps** — discovered mid-engagement | Medium | Medium | Pre-engagement questionnaire surfaces data and tool access issues before kickoff; readiness gaps documented in Assessment and factored into Blueprint timeline |
| **AI tool landscape shifts** — a platform we've built on breaks, changes pricing, or loses capability | High | Medium | Vendor-agnostic approach; architecture designed to be portable where possible; Claude API and n8n are primary stack (open-source n8n reduces vendor lock-in) |
| **Founder capacity bottleneck** — too much work for one person | Medium | Medium | Subcontractor relationships established in months 1–3; clear criteria for when to bring in help; cap concurrent Tier 3 builds at 2 until team grows |
| **Long sales cycles** — SMBs deliberate slowly | Medium | High | Low-commitment, fixed-fee Tier 1 entry point removes the biggest barrier; pilot pricing for first clients de-risks further; retainer revenue smooths monthly cash flow |
| **Client dissatisfaction with agent performance** | Low | High | 2 UAT review rounds before launch; 30-day hypercare included in every Tier 3 build; KPIs defined and agreed before build begins |
| **Competition from AI-native platforms adding SMB services** | High | Low-Med | Methodology, trust, and SMB-specific delivery are not easily replicated by platforms; relationships and reputation are durable advantages |
| **Founder illness or unavailability** | Low | High | Maintain a subcontractor relationship who could step in for critical delivery; client communication protocols documented; avoid single points of failure in any running agent |

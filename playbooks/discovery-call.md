# Discovery Call Playbook
**Forge Solutions — Internal Use**

30 minutes. Video. No deck, no pitch. The goal is mutual: figure out if working together makes sense, and if so, where to start.

---

## Pre-Call (5 min before)

- Review their booking form responses if available
- Do a 2-min scan: company website, LinkedIn, any news
- Know their industry and rough size going in
- Have this doc open — not to read from, but to keep you on track
- Set a 25-min soft timer so you have 5 min to wrap

---

## Opening (2–3 min)

Keep it brief. They're sizing you up the same way you're sizing them up.

> "Thanks for making time. Here's how I run these — I'm going to ask you a lot of questions about your business and how things actually work. No slides from me, no pitch. At the end I'll tell you honestly whether I think there's a fit and what I'd recommend. Sound good?"

Establish the pace early. You're running this, not them.

---

## Business Context (4–5 min)

Get oriented fast. You need to understand what the business actually does before you can assess automation opportunities.

**Ask:**
- "Give me the quick version — what does the business do, how big is the team, and how is the work organized?"
- "Who are your customers and how do they find you?"
- "What does a normal week look like operationally — what's happening every day?"

**Listen for:**
- Repetitive, high-volume transactional work
- Manual coordination across people or systems
- Any mention of "we do it in spreadsheets" or "someone has to remember to"
- Whether ops is centralized or scattered across people

---

## Workflow and Pain Exploration (12–15 min)

This is the core of the call. You're looking for the specific friction — not vague AI interest.

**Opening probe:**
> "Walk me through the workflows that eat the most time or cause the most headaches. Not what you wish you could automate — just where things slow down or break."

**Follow-up questions (use as needed, not all of them):**
- "How long does that take right now, per occurrence? How often does it happen?"
- "Who does it — one person or multiple people?"
- "What triggers it? What does it produce at the end?"
- "Where does it live? What tools are involved?"
- "What happens when it goes wrong or gets delayed?"
- "Has anyone tried to fix this before? What happened?"
- "Is the data you work with mostly structured — spreadsheets, forms — or is it freeform?"

**Scoring while you listen** *(mental model, not a form you fill out on the call)*:

| Signal | Good | Caution |
|--------|------|---------|
| Volume | High-frequency, recurring | One-off or rare |
| Pain clarity | Can name the specific bottleneck | Vague frustration with AI generally |
| Data state | Structured, in systems | Manual, paper, inconsistent |
| Internal owner | Someone responsible | No one clearly owns it |
| Business impact | Time or revenue tied to it | Peripheral to core ops |

**Watch for the telling detail.** The best opportunities come out in throwaway phrases: *"we have someone who just does that all day"* or *"we lose bids because we're too slow on estimates"* or *"I have to remind everyone every single week."*

---

## Prior Attempts (2–3 min)

Tells you a lot about maturity and realistic expectations.

- "Have you tried to address any of this before? Software, contractors, internal projects?"
- "What happened?"
- "What's your current read on AI — have you experimented with anything?"

**Why this matters:**
- If they've tried and failed, understand why before you propose the same thing
- If they've never tried, assess whether they have realistic expectations
- If they're already using AI tools, understand the context — you may be augmenting not replacing

---

## Fit Assessment (3–4 min)

Be direct. This is where you earn credibility.

**If it's a fit:**
> "Here's my read. You've got [specific workflow] — that's a real automation candidate. The pattern you're describing maps to what I work on regularly. Here's what I'd recommend as a starting point..."

**If it's not a fit yet:**
> "Honestly, I don't think you're quite at the point where engaging me makes sense yet, and here's why. What I'd suggest instead is..."

**If it needs more discovery before you can assess:**
> "I don't have enough to give you a confident recommendation yet — there's too much I don't know about [X]. The Tier 1 Assessment is actually designed to answer exactly that question. Here's what it produces and what it costs..."

---

## Tier Recommendation

| Situation | Recommend |
|-----------|-----------|
| Not sure where AI fits, multiple workflows to evaluate | **Tier 1 — Assessment** |
| Specific use case identified, want a plan before building | **Tier 2 — Blueprint** |
| Well-scoped use case, ready to invest | **Tier 3 — Build** |
| Already has agents deployed, needs ongoing support | **Tier 4 — Retainer** |
| Has existing agents, suspects problems | **Add-on — Agent Audit** |
| Not ready / not a fit | Refer out honestly |

---

## Next Steps (2 min)

Be specific or don't close anything. Vague next steps produce ghosting.

**If recommending Tier 1:**
> "What I'd suggest is a Tier 1 Assessment. It's $2,500–$5,000 depending on scope, takes 1–2 weeks, and gives you a prioritized roadmap before you spend anything on building. The report is yours to keep regardless. Want me to send over what that looks like in detail?"

**If sending a proposal:**
> "I'll put together a short scope document and send it over by [day]. Turnaround from signed SOW to first session is typically within a week."

**Always:**
- Confirm email to follow up to
- Set a specific timeframe for your follow-up ("I'll send something by Thursday")
- Don't leave it open-ended

---

## Green Flags

Strong signals that this is a good engagement:

- Can name a specific bottleneck with volume/time attached to it
- Someone internally is championing this — not just "the CEO said we should do AI"
- Already tracks data in some form, even imperfect
- Realistic about timeline and investment (not expecting miracles in a week for $500)
- Leadership is engaged but not micromanaging the process
- Has already felt the pain of *not* solving this (missed revenue, employee frustration, errors)

---

## Red Flags

Proceed with caution or pass:

- Wants AI because it's trendy — can't articulate a specific problem
- Expects full automation of complex workflows for a minimal budget
- No one internally owns the process being discussed
- IT/CTO has already mandated a specific tool and wants you to just build in it
- Wants to skip discovery and jump straight to building
- Resistance framing: "my team will never adopt this" said with resignation rather than as a challenge to solve
- Scope is too large for the budget they're describing

---

## After the Call (same day)

- Send a follow-up email within 4 hours — reference something specific from the call
- If recommending an engagement: attach the relevant tier one-pager or link to services page
- If sending a proposal: note the scope you discussed and when to expect it
- If not a fit: say it clearly, point them somewhere useful, and leave the door open for later

**Log the call in prospects.json** with:
- Company name, contact, industry, size
- Key workflows discussed
- Recommended tier and why
- Fit score (1–10) and your notes
- Follow-up action and deadline

# AI Readiness Assessment — Process Guide
**Forge Solutions — Internal Use**

**Scope:** Tier 1 engagement · 1–2 weeks · $2,500–$5,000
**Deliverable:** AI Agent Opportunity Report (10–20 pages) + live readout

This is not a survey or a checkbox exercise. It's structured discovery — you're building a picture of how the business actually operates, where the real friction is, and what's genuinely automatable vs. what just sounds automatable. The output is only as good as the sessions that feed it.

---

## Engagement Overview

| Phase | Activity | Time |
|-------|----------|------|
| Pre-engagement | Intake questionnaire sent + reviewed | Before Session 1 |
| Session 1 | Landscape mapping — workflows, pain, context | 60–90 min |
| Session 2 | Deep-dive process mapping (selected workflows) | 60–90 min |
| Session 3 (if needed) | Validation + opportunity scoring | 45–60 min |
| Between sessions | Process map drafts, scoring, report drafting | Your time |
| Delivery | AI Agent Opportunity Report sent + 48hr review window | — |
| Readout | Live walkthrough + Q&A | 60 min |

**Focused scope ($2,500):** 3 workflows, 1–2 departments — Sessions 1 + 2 only
**Standard scope ($3,750):** 5 workflows, 2–3 departments — Sessions 1 + 2 + partial 3
**Comprehensive scope ($5,000):** Up to 8 workflows, 3–5 departments — Full 3 sessions

## Pre-Engagement: Intake Questionnaire

Send 48 hours before Session 1. Keep it short — you want answers, not an essay.

---

*Send as email or shared doc. Adapt the intro to match your voice.*

**Subject:** Quick prep for our AI Readiness sessions — [Company Name]

> Before our first session, please take 15–20 minutes to fill this out. There are no right answers — I want your honest read on where things stand. The more specific you are, the more useful our sessions will be.

---

**Section 1 — Business Operations**

1. Describe your core operations in 3–5 sentences. What does the team do every day?
2. How many people are involved in operations, and how is work currently organized (teams, roles)?
3. What are your top 3 priorities as a business over the next 12 months?

**Section 2 — Workflows and Pain Points**

4. List the 5–8 workflows or tasks that consume the most staff time. (Examples: intake processing, report generation, scheduling, follow-ups, data entry, billing, compliance checks)
5. Which of those workflows breaks down most often, takes the longest, or causes the most frustration? Why?
6. Where do errors happen most frequently? What's the downstream impact when they do?
7. Are there any tasks currently done by someone whose time you feel is wasted on that task?

**Section 3 — Data and Systems**

8. List the tools and software your team uses daily. (CRM, PM tools, communication, accounting, industry-specific tools)
9. Where does your data live — spreadsheets, databases, a CRM, PDFs, email? How structured is it?
10. Is there data that's captured inconsistently or not captured at all, that you wish you had?

**Section 4 — Prior Experience**

11. Have you tried to automate or streamline any of these workflows before? What happened?
12. Is anyone on the team currently using AI tools (ChatGPT, Copilot, etc.)? For what?
13. What's the general attitude toward adopting new technology on your team — enthusiastic, cautious, resistant?

**Section 5 — Success and Constraints**

14. What would a successful outcome of this engagement look like to you in one sentence?
15. Are there workflows that are off-limits for automation — for compliance, trust, or other reasons?
16. Who are the 1–2 people on your team who best understand how the most painful workflows actually work? (These are the people we want in our sessions.)

## Session 1 — Landscape Mapping (60–90 min)

**Goal:** Build a shared picture of the business and identify which 3–8 workflows to map in depth.

**Who should be in the room:** Founder/operator + the 1–2 people who actually do the work. Avoid having only leadership present.

### Agenda

**Opening (5 min)**
> "My goal today is to understand how your business actually operates — not the org chart version, the real version. I'm going to ask a lot of questions. Some will seem obvious. Answer them anyway because I'm building context, not testing you."

**Business and team walkthrough (15 min)**
- Walk me through a typical week. What happens Monday morning? What's the rhythm?
- How does work come in? (Leads, jobs, cases, orders — whatever the entry point is)
- How does it get done? Who touches it and in what order?
- How does it leave? (Delivery, billing, closure — whatever the exit point is)

**Workflow inventory (20–25 min)**

Go through their intake questionnaire answers together. For each workflow they listed:
- Is this daily, weekly, monthly? What volume?
- How long does it take per instance?
- Who does it?
- What does it produce?
- Where does it hurt?

Build a rough list. Don't go deep yet — you're cataloging, not mapping.

**Prioritization (15 min)**

Together, select the workflows to map in Session 2. Aim for the intersection of:
- High frequency / high time cost
- Clear pain point or error rate
- Willing to change it (not sacred)
- Data exists to work with

> "I want to spend our next session going deep on the workflows with the best combination of impact and feasibility. Based on what you've told me, my instinct is [X and Y]. Do those feel right?"

**Wrap (5 min)**
- Confirm which workflows you're mapping in Session 2
- Confirm who should be in the room for Session 2 (ideally the people who do the specific workflows)
- Send a simple process map template beforehand so they can sketch anything in advance if they want

## Session 2 — Process Mapping (60–90 min)

**Goal:** Build detailed process maps for the selected workflows. You're doing this together, live — not asking them to fill out a form.

**Who should be in the room:** The people who actually do each workflow being mapped. This may mean different people for different workflows.

### For each workflow, capture:

```
Workflow Name:
Trigger: What starts it? (email received, form submitted, calendar date, etc.)
Owner: Who is responsible?
Volume: How many times per [day/week/month]?
Avg. Duration: How long per instance?

Steps (number each one):
  1. [Action] → [Tool/System] → [Output]
  2. ...

Decision Points: Where is human judgment applied? What are the options?
Handoffs: Where does work pass from one person to another?
Pain Points: Where does it slow down, break, or require rework?
Error Rate: How often does something go wrong here? What happens?

Data Inputs: What information is needed to complete this workflow?
  - Format: [structured/unstructured/mixed]
  - Source: [where does it live]
  - Quality: [clean / inconsistent / often missing]

Data Outputs: What does the completed workflow produce?
  - Format:
  - Destination:

Integration Points: What systems does this workflow touch?
Systems Used: [list each]

Notes / Context:
```

### Facilitation technique

Don't let them describe it abstractly. Walk them through it step-by-step, live.

> "Let's do a real one. Walk me through the last time you actually did this. Start from the beginning — something triggered it. What was that?"

Then follow the thread. When they skip a step: "What happened before that?" When they're vague: "What does that actually mean — who does what, in what system?"

Draw it out as they talk. Share your screen if that helps. The goal is a map they'd recognize as accurate when you send it back.

### Red flags to note during mapping

- "Someone just knows to do that" — undocumented tribal knowledge, automation risk
- "It depends" on everything — high variability, complexity may exceed ROI
- "We've been meaning to fix this" — known problem with no owner or momentum
- Inputs that are PDFs, emails, or freeform text with no structure — data prep may dominate effort
- "We use [specific enterprise system] for that" — integration complexity and licensing constraints

## Session 3 — Validation and Scoring (45–60 min)
*(Comprehensive scope, or when needed for Standard)*

**Goal:** Review the process maps for accuracy, surface anything missed, and score opportunities together.

### Agenda

**Process map review (20 min)**
- Walk through each map you've drafted
- "Does this match how it actually works? What did I miss?"
- Capture any corrections and additions

**Opportunity scoring (20 min)**
Walk through the scoring rubric together for each workflow. Don't make this a spreadsheet exercise — it's a conversation. Their input on strategic fit and data readiness is particularly valuable.

**Constraint discussion (10 min)**
- What's the appetite for change? Where will there be resistance?
- Are there compliance, privacy, or security considerations for any of these?
- What integrations would require IT involvement or vendor approval?
- Budget range they're comfortable discussing at this stage?

## Opportunity Scoring Rubric

Score each workflow on four dimensions (1–5 each). Maximum score: 20.

### 1 — ROI Potential
*How much value does automating this create?*

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **5** | High volume + high time cost + outcome is clearly measurable (hours saved, errors eliminated, revenue captured). The math is obvious. |
| **4** | Good savings likely; volume and time cost are meaningful; outcome is measurable with some effort |
| **3** | Moderate savings; medium volume; measurable but not dramatic |
| **2** | Possible savings but magnitude is uncertain; low volume or hard to measure |
| **1** | Speculative; peripheral to core operations; hard to attribute impact |

### 2 — Implementation Complexity
*How hard is this to build? (Higher score = simpler to build)*

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **5** | Structured inputs/outputs; 0–1 integrations; standard workflow pattern; minimal edge cases |
| **4** | Mostly clear; 1–2 integrations needed; a few edge cases; well-understood pattern |
| **3** | Multi-step workflow; 2–3 integrations; some decision logic; moderate customization |
| **2** | Complex logic or messy data; 3–4 integrations; significant edge cases; custom architecture needed |
| **1** | Unstructured inputs; 4+ integrations; high variability; custom everything; significant research required |

### 3 — Data Readiness
*Is the data in a state that supports automation?*

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **5** | Clean, structured, accessible via API or export; consistent format; no prep work needed |
| **4** | Mostly structured; minor cleanup needed; accessible through reasonable effort |
| **3** | Data exists but needs cleanup; partially structured; some manual extraction or transformation required |
| **2** | Scattered across systems; inconsistent formats; significant data prep work required before automation is viable |
| **1** | Not systematically captured; paper/email/manual; major data infrastructure work needed first |

### 4 — Strategic Fit
*Does leadership want this solved? Is the org ready?*

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **5** | Directly tied to a business priority; strong leadership support; clear internal owner; team ready to adopt |
| **4** | High operational value; good support; clear owner; one or two adoption considerations to manage |
| **3** | Useful but not a stated priority; moderate support; owner identified but not driving it |
| **2** | Peripheral to strategy; limited enthusiasm; no clear champion; likely to deprioritize |
| **1** | Doesn't align with current direction; resistance expected; leadership hasn't committed to addressing it |

### Score Interpretation

| Total | Recommendation |
|-------|---------------|
| **16–20** | **Prioritize.** Strong candidate for immediate action. Lead with this. |
| **11–15** | **Queue it.** Good opportunity — plan for next 3–6 months or alongside the top priority. |
| **6–10** | **Revisit later.** Address data or strategic gaps first; re-assess in 6–12 months. |
| **1–5** | **Not yet.** Underlying issues need to be solved before automation is viable. Note the blockers clearly. |

## Report: AI Agent Opportunity Report

The report is 10–20 pages depending on scope. It should be something the client keeps and uses — not a pretty PDF they file away.

Write it plainly. Short sentences. Specific numbers where you have them. No consultant-speak.

### Structure

**1. Executive Summary (1 page)**
- What you assessed (scope, workflows, sessions)
- Top 3 recommendations in one sentence each
- Implementation sequence at a glance
- Total estimated effort and cost range (rough order of magnitude)

**2. Business Context (½–1 page)**
- What the business does and how it operates
- Why AI agent automation is relevant to them right now
- Any relevant constraints or considerations

**3. Workflows Assessed (1 page per workflow)**
For each:
- Process map (diagram or step list — whichever communicates more clearly)
- Current state summary (who, what, how long, how often, error rate)
- Key pain points
- Opportunity scoring table
- What automation would look like at a high level

**4. Opportunity Scorecard (1 page)**
Summary table of all workflows scored, sorted by total score. Visual.

**5. Recommendations (1–1.5 pages per recommendation)**
Present the top 3 prioritized opportunities:
- What to automate and why (specific, not generic)
- What the automation would do — inputs, logic, outputs
- What it connects to (systems and integrations required)
- Rough effort estimate: small (1–2 weeks), medium (3–6 weeks), large (8–12 weeks)
- Rough cost range (use tier pricing as reference)
- Expected ROI: time saved per month, error reduction, revenue impact if applicable
- Key risks or dependencies to address

**6. Implementation Sequence (1 page)**
A simple phased roadmap:
- Phase 1 (Now): Highest-score, lowest-complexity recommendation
- Phase 2 (3–6 months): Second priority
- Phase 3 (6–12 months): Third priority or revisit lower-scoring items
- Prerequisites: What needs to happen before each phase can start

**7. Constraints and Considerations (½ page)**
Be honest here. Call out:
- Data gaps that need to be addressed
- Integration complexity that may affect cost
- Change management considerations (team readiness, adoption)
- Compliance or security notes
- Dependencies on third parties (vendors, IT)

**8. Next Steps (½ page)**
Specific. Not "consider a pilot." Say exactly what the client should do next and what working with you looks like from here.

## Report Quality Standards

Before you send it:

- [ ] Every recommendation includes a rough cost range — not "TBD"
- [ ] Every process map was reviewed for accuracy with the client
- [ ] Opportunity scores are explained, not just listed
- [ ] The sequence is justified — why this order?
- [ ] Executive summary can stand alone — a reader who only reads page 1 knows the essential findings
- [ ] No buzzwords: "leverage AI," "AI-powered," "unlock potential" — delete all of it
- [ ] A client who takes this report to another vendor has everything they need to act on it

---

## Readout Session (60 min)

**Don't just walk through the report slide by slide.**

Open with the headline:
> "Here's what I found. Your top opportunity is [X] — here's why, and here's what doing something about it actually looks like."

Then go deeper as questions arise. Keep it conversational.

**Typical agenda:**
- Top 3 findings (15 min)
- Implementation sequence and rationale (10 min)
- Q&A / drill-down on specific workflows (20 min)
- Constraints and honest caveats (5 min)
- Next steps discussion (10 min)

**End with a specific ask:**
If you're recommending a Tier 2 Blueprint — say so, say what it costs, and say how long it takes. Don't leave it open-ended.
